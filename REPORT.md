Task 4 — A better leaf classifier

This study evaluates 13 variants of a leaf classification pipeline based on 101 training images to maximize accuracy through a smartphone camera. Each variant isolated a single modification relative to a baseline CNN, evaluated across three random seeds and three distinct test sets (validation, camera-simulated, and historical out-of-distribution).
Pretrained Representations were replacing the custom CNN with an ImageNet-pretrained MobileNet backbone yielded the most significant performance gain, raising camera-simulated accuracy from 0.393 to 0.670.About feature freezing vs. fine-tuning were training only the final classification layer while freezing the MobileNet backbone (v2a-frozen) achieved the same validation accuracy (0.702) as full fine-tuning but with drastically lower variance across seeds ($\pm 0.036$ vs. $\pm 0.125$), preventing overfitting on the small dataset.For augmentation and data diversity the incorporating color jitter (v1-color) improved camera robustness (0.485). Similarly, adding 700 historical images (v5-history) boosted camera accuracy to 0.490, demonstrating that data diversity outperforms algorithmic regularization tricks.In the end the standard regularization techniques failed or degraded performance on this small-scale dataset was dropout (0.5) and Global Average Pooling: Severely under-fitted the data by discarding spatial structure or over-restricting capacity.Also AdamW and Label Smoothing were Yielded negligible changes within the statistical noise threshold and Combined Regularization (v4e-combined) were stacking multiple regularizers resulted in lower performance than the unregularized baseline (0.379 camera accuracy).So The optimal model selected for mobile deployment is v3-schedule (MobileNet fine-tuned with geometric and color augmentation, Adam optimizer, and cosine annealing over 40 epochs).It achieved the highest camera-simulated accuracy (0.705) and out-of-distribution generalisation (0.625), outperforming the previous model by over 11% on camera data. Because it retains the same MobileNet architecture, it maintains an identical footprint (~6 MB ONNX) for direct integration into the mobile application.
